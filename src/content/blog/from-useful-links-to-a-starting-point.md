There are many useful resources, tools, communities and projects for data-driven research in life sciences and health. Some are connected to ELIXIR, others to TDCC LSH, national communities or international initiatives.

Listing these resources is not enough. A long list of links does not always help people decide where to start.

## The need

People usually come with a practical question, so they need a simple way to get from that question to a useful starting point. For example

- **I want to share or manage data.** Start with guidance on research data management, FAIR implementation or metadata standards.
- **I need a tool or workflow.** Look at tool registries, workflow platforms or training resources.
- **I want to connect to others.** Find a relevant community, network or expert group.
- **I want to see what is already happening.** Explore related projects and examples.

## What was used

The TDCC LSH flyer brought together a small set of existing resources, communities and project examples. These included RDMkit, FAIR Metroline, FAIRsharing, bio.tools, Galaxy, WorkflowHub, TeSS, the Dutch Interoperability Network, BioSB, ELIXIR NL, the Data Steward Interest Group and TDCC LSH project initiatives.

## What was done

The resources were grouped around practical user needs instead of being listed one after another. The flyer became a compact entry point for meetings, events and project conversations. It pointed people to existing resources and communities rather than creating new guidance.

## What changed

The flyer made it easier to show the range of support that already exists. It also showed that visibility takes more than sharing links. Resources become more useful when they are connected to the questions people actually have.

## Lessons learned

- A useful resource overview starts from user questions, not from the organisations behind the resources.
- A simple product like a flyer can help people find a first direction. It works best when the categories are practical and recognisable.
- The next step is to make this more interactive and story-based, so people can see where to click and also why a resource, community or project might be relevant to them.
